Dart Scoreboard is made by Jon Hill. This page explains what the app does with information, in plain language.

What stays on your devices and in your iCloud

Your games, players, photos, stats and settings are stored on your device and, if you’re signed in to iCloud, in your own iCloud account, where Apple’s privacy protections apply. I can’t see them.

When you play a game across devices, that game is shared through iCloud with the people you invite or who join with your code, and they see the players’ names and photos in that game. When an Apple TV hosts a game, a small record with the game’s name, the TV’s name and a four-digit code is kept in the app’s public iCloud database for a few hours so phones can find it. It contains nothing about you.

Usage counts

The app sends anonymous usage counts to TelemetryDeck, a privacy-focused analytics service based in Germany: which kinds of games are played and how features are used, along with the app version, device model and system version. No names, scores or personal details are sent, and the identifier used is a salted hash that can’t be traced back to you. TelemetryDeck’s privacy policy: https://telemetrydeck.com/privacy/

Purchases

Dart Scoreboard Pro subscriptions are handled by Apple. To keep track of what you’ve bought, the app uses RevenueCat, which receives your App Store purchase receipt and an anonymous identifier, never your name or Apple Account. RevenueCat’s privacy policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy/

Feedback

If you send feedback from the app, the email comes from your own mail app and includes the app version and your device model so I can help. I use it only to reply to you.

No ads, no tracking, no accounts

The app has no advertising, no third-party tracking, and no accounts to create. It doesn’t collect your location or contacts, and the only photos it touches are the player photos you choose, which stay in your iCloud.

Children

The app isn’t directed at children under 13 and collects no personal information from anyone.
